## Tell us about a problem you've noticed around you that you would genuinely like to solve. Why does it matter?

One thing I keep noticing, and honestly it bothers me a lot, is that our country has always been "good" in tech but never really a "superpower" in any single niche field like AI. We have brilliant people, some of the best engineers in the world actually work here or come from here, but somehow we're always a few steps behind USA and China when it comes to actually leading in AI research and cutting edge tech. I don't fully understand all the reasons behind this yet (maybe its funding, maybe its infrastructure, maybe its just scale), but I know I want to be part of changing that.

I've wanted to work towards this for a few years now, even before I properly knew what "AI" meant beyond just a buzzword. I feel like if AI is actually implemented properly in India, at ground level and not just in flashy startups, we could fix a lot of everyday problems that we've just learned to live with. A simple example is littering, imagine smart camera systems using something like computer vision to detect and report littering in public spaces automatically, or waste segregation systems that use AI to sort garbage properly instead of relying on manual labour which often doesn't happen efficiently. These sound like small problems compared to "make India an AI superpower" but I genuinely believe superpowers are built by solving a lot of small real problems, not just one big breakthrough.

This matters to me because I don't want to just consume technology that other countries build, I want to be someone who contributes to building it here, for our own problems, in our own context.

## Describe something you've learned outside a classroom or formal course. How did you learn it?

I taught myself **Python**, **C**, and basic **Linux commands**, mostly through a mix of books and YouTube tutorials rather than any structured course. It wasn't the smoothest process honestly, there were a lot of moments where I'd follow a tutorial, get an error, have no idea why, and just sit there googling the exact error message hoping Stack Overflow had an answer (it usually did).

With Linux specifically, I remember being scared to even open the terminal at first because it felt like one wrong command could break everything. But slowly, just by using it regularly, trying out commands, occasionally breaking things and having to figure out how to fix them, I got comfortable with it. I think that's honestly the biggest lesson I learned outside of any classroom: you don't really learn tech by just reading about it, you learn it by breaking things and fixing them yourself.
